## Basic Concept of Conics and Eccentricity

**Conics are mainly of 5 types:**
* Circle
* Ellipse
* Parabola
* Hyperbola
* Pair of Straight Lines

### Eccentricity ($e$)
> * The tendency to bend.
> * A measure of how much it deviates from a circular shape.
> * According to Physics, the tendency to spread out (diverge).

![[assets/conics_basic_definition.png]] [Diagram: Focus S(α, β), directrix lx+my+n=0, and moving point P(x, y)]

Here,
* $S(\alpha, \beta) \rightarrow$ Fixed point / Focus
* $lx + my + n = 0 \rightarrow$ Directrix (imaginary line)
* $P(x, y) \rightarrow$ Moving point on the conic

$$\boxed{\enspace e = \frac{SP}{PM} \enspace}$$

> [!tip]- Proof (Derivation of the General Equation of a Conic)
> By definition,
> 
> $$
> e = \frac{SP}{PM} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad SP = e \cdot PM
> $$
> 
> According to the figure, substituting the distance from the focus $S(\alpha, \beta)$ to $P(x, y)$ and the perpendicular distance from the directrix $lx + my + n = 0$ to $P(x, y)$, we get,
> 
> $$
> \sqrt{(x-\alpha)^2 + (y-\beta)^2} = e \left| \frac{lx + my + n}{\sqrt{l^2 + m^2}} \right|
> $$
> 
> Squaring both sides, we get,
> 
> $$
> (x-\alpha)^2 + (y-\beta)^2 = e^2 \frac{(lx + my + n)^2}{l^2 + m^2}
> $$
> 
> Simplifying, we get,
> 
> $$
> \boxed{\enspace ax^2 + by^2 + 2hxy + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 \enspace}
> $$
> 
> This is the general or standard equation of a conic.

### Values of Eccentricity for Different Conics
As a conic transitions from a circle to an ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, and straight line, the directrix gradually moves closer from infinity ($\infty$), and in the case of a straight line, it lies right on the line itself, resulting in $PM = 0$.

| Name of Conic | Distance Condition | Mathematical Form ($e = \frac{SP}{PM}$) | Value of Eccentricity ($e$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Circle** | $PM = \infty$ | $$e = \frac{SP}{\infty}$$ | $$\boxed{e = 0}$$ |
| **Ellipse** | $PM > SP$ | $$e = \frac{SP}{PM} < 1$$ | $$\boxed{0 < e < 1}$$ |
| **Parabola** | $SP = PM$ | $$e = \frac{SP}{PM} = 1$$ | $$\boxed{e = 1}$$ |
| **Hyperbola** | $SP > PM$ | $$e = \frac{SP}{PM} > 1$$ | $$\boxed{e > 1}$$ |
| **Straight Line** | $PM = 0$ | $$e = \frac{SP}{0}$$ | $$\boxed{e = \infty}$$ |

## Identification of Conics

General equation of a conic:
$$ax^2 + by^2 + 2hxy + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$$

Discriminant (Determinant),
$$
\Delta = \begin{vmatrix} a & h & g \\ h & b & f \\ g & f & c \end{vmatrix}
$$

> [!note] Trick to Remember the Determinant
> Place $a, b, c$ along the principal diagonal. Then place $h, g$ symmetrically in the 1st row and 1st column, and fill the remaining empty spaces with $f$ to form the determinant.

1. **If $\Delta = 0$:** The equation represents a **pair of straight lines**.
2. **If $\Delta \neq 0$:** The equation represents a **curve** (circle, ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola):
   * **Circle:** $a = b$ and $h = 0$
   * **Parabola:** $ab = h^2$ or $ab - h^2 = 0$
   * **Ellipse:** $ab > h^2$ or $ab - h^2 > 0$
   * **Hyperbola:** $ab < h^2$ or $ab - h^2 < 0$

> [!note] Tricks to Remember the Conditions
> * The expression $ab - h^2$ is equivalent to $(1 - e^2)$.
> * For example, in a parabola: $ab - h^2 = 0 \implies 1 - e^2 = 0 \implies e^2 = 1 \implies e = 1$. Thus, knowing the value of $e$ makes it easy to remember which conic corresponds to $ab - h^2 > 0$ and which to $ab - h^2 < 0$.
> * Calculate $ab$ and $h^2$ only when $\Delta \neq 0$. If $\Delta = 0$, regardless of the values of $ab$ and $h^2$, it will always be a **pair of straight lines**.
> * **MCQ Tricks:** Pair of straight lines rarely appears outside of admission tests. If "pair of straight lines" is not among the options, there is no need to calculate $\Delta$; directly compute $ab$ and $h^2$. Since $h^2$ is always positive ($+ve$), if $ab$ is negative ($-ve$) or $0$ (when $h \neq 0$), it is a **hyperbola**. And if $ab$ is positive ($+ve$), it will be an **ellipse** provided $ab > h^2$.

> [!important] 01: What type of conic is $x^2 - 5xy + 6y^2 = 0$?
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Comparing the given equation with the general equation, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > 1\cdot x^2 + 6y^2 + 2\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)xy + 2\cdot 0\cdot x + 2\cdot 0\cdot y + 0 = 0
> > $$
> > 
> > Here,
> > 
> > $$
> > \Delta = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & -\frac{5}{2} & 0 \\ -\frac{5}{2} & 6 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = 0
> > $$
> > 
> > Since $\Delta = 0$, it represents a **pair of straight lines**.
> > 
> > **Alternative (By Factorization):**
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > x^2 - 5xy + 6y^2 &= 0 \\
> > \Rightarrow (x - 2y)(x - 3y) &= 0
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, $x = 2y$ and $x = 3y$, which are equations of two straight lines.

> [!important] 02: What does $y = x - \frac{1}{x}$ represent?
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Given equation,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > y &= x - \frac{1}{x} \\
> > \Rightarrow xy &= x^2 - 1 \\
> > \Rightarrow x^2 + 0\cdot y^2 + 2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)xy - 1 &= 0
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Here, $ab = 1 \times 0 = 0$ and $h^2 = \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}$.
> > 
> > Since $ab < h^2 \left(0 < \frac{1}{4}\right)$, it is a **hyperbola**.

> [!important] 03: What does $2x^2 - 3y^2 + xy + 6x - 9y + 1 = 0$ represent?
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Here, $a = 2, \enspace b = -3, \enspace h = \frac{1}{2}$
> > 
> > Therefore,
> > 
> > $$
> > ab = 2 \times (-3) = -6 \qquad \text{and} \qquad h^2 = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}
> > $$
> > 
> > Since $ab < h^2$, it is a **hyperbola**.

## Parabola

![[assets/parabola_four_forms.png]] [Diagram: Figures of the four standard forms of parabola (y²=4ax, y²=-4ax, x²=4ay, x²=-4ay)]

> [!note] Characteristics of a Standard Parabola
> 1. Between $x$ and $y$, if the power (degree) of one is $1$ and the other is $2$, it is a standard parabola.
> 2. The axis corresponding to the variable with power $1$ divides the parabola into two equal halves (axis of symmetry).
> 3. Regardless of the direction of the focus, the distance between the vertex and the focus is always $|a|$, and the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the directrix is also $|a|$.
> 4. The direction in which the parabola opens depends on the sign of $a$:
>    * If $a$ is positive ($+ve$), it opens to the right or upwards.
>    * If $a$ is negative ($-ve$), it opens to the left or downwards.
> 5. The directrix lies on the side opposite to the direction in which the parabola opens.
> 6. The axis and the directrix are perpendicular to each other.

### Derivation of the Standard Equation of a Parabola

![[assets/parabola_derivation.png]] [Diagram: Focus S(a,0), vertex (0,0), and directrix x+a=0 of the parabola y² = 4ax]

$$\boxed{\enspace y^2 = 4ax \enspace}$$

> [!tip]- Proof
> By the definition of a parabola ($e = 1$), we always have
> 
> $$
> SP = PM
> $$
> 
> In the figure, the focus is $S(a, 0)$ and the equation of the directrix is $x = -a$ or $x + a = 0$. Therefore,
> 
> $$
> \sqrt{(x-a)^2 + (y-0)^2} = \left| \frac{x + a}{\sqrt{1^2 + 0^2}} \right|
> $$
> 
> Squaring both sides, we get,
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> (x-a)^2 + y^2 &= (x+a)^2 \\
> \Rightarrow y^2 &= (x+a)^2 - (x-a)^2 \\
> \Rightarrow y^2 &= 4ax
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> 
> This is the equation of a standard parabola.

### Proof of the Length of the Latus Rectum

![[assets/parabola_latus_rectum.png]] [Diagram: Latus rectum of a parabola and its extremities (a, 2a) and (a, -2a)]

> [!tip]- Proof
> According to the figure, if $SP$ is the semi-latus rectum passing through the focus $S(a, 0)$, then
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{Length of the latus rectum} &= 2 \cdot SP \\
> &= 2 \cdot PM \\
> &= 2 \cdot ZS \\
> &= 2 \times 2a = 4a
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> 
> The coordinates of the extremities of the latus rectum are $(a, 2a)$ and $(a, -2a)$.

### Formulas for Standard Parabolas

| Property                                     | $y^2 = 4ax$             | $x^2 = 4ay$             |
| :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------- | :---------------------- |
| **Vertex**                                   | $(0, 0)$                | $(0, 0)$                |
| **Focus ($S$)**                              | $(a, 0)$                | $(0, a)$                |
| **Intersection of Axis and Directrix ($Z$)** | $(-a, 0)$               | $(0, -a)$               |
| **Equation of the Axis**                     | $y = 0$ ($x$-axis)      | $x = 0$ ($y$-axis)      |
| **Equation of the Directrix**                | $x = -a$ or $x + a = 0$ | $y = -a$ or $y + a = 0$ |
| **Equation of Tangent at the Vertex**        | $x = 0$                 | $y = 0$                 |
| **Length of the Latus Rectum**               | $4a$                    | $4a$                    |
| **Extremities of the Latus Rectum**          | $(a, \pm 2a)$           | $(\pm 2a, a)$           |

## Sketching Parabolas and Shifting of Graphs

![[assets/parabola_graph_shifting.png]] [Diagram: Graphs of y = -√x, y = √(x-1), and y = -(x-3)²]

* **Graph of $y = -\sqrt{x}$:** Squaring both sides gives $y^2 = x$. However, since the original equation is $y = -\sqrt{x}$, $y$ can never be positive ($+ve$), and being inside the square root, $x$ is always non-negative ($+ve$). Thus, its graph consists only of the lower half in the 4th quadrant.
* **Graph of $y = \sqrt{x-1}$:** Here $y$ is always non-negative ($+ve$). Squaring both sides, we get,
  $$y^2 = x - 1 \implies (y-0)^2 = 4 \cdot \frac{1}{4}(x-1)$$
  Here the vertex is $(1, 0)$ and the graph undergoes a right shift (only the upper branch in the 1st quadrant).
* **Graph of $y = -(x-3)^2$:**
  $$(x-3)^2 = -y \implies (x-3)^2 = 4\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right)(y-0)$$
  Here the vertex is $(3, 0)$ and since $a$ is negative, the graph opens downwards.

> [!note] Graph Shifting and MCQ Shortcuts
> * In any graph, replacing $x$ with $(x-a)$ shifts the original graph $a$ units to the right along the $x$-axis. Replacing $x$ with $(x+a)$ shifts it $a$ units to the left.
> * Convert any parabola's equation into the form $(x-\alpha)^2 = 4a(y-\beta)$ or $(y-\beta)^2 = 4a(x-\alpha)$. Its vertex will be $(\alpha, \beta)$. Then let $X = x-\alpha$ and $Y = y-\beta$ and directly apply the standard formulas.
> * **Latus Rectum Shortcut:** In the equation of a parabola, if the coefficient of the quadratic (degree $2$) variable is made $1$, the absolute value of the coefficient of the linear (degree $1$) variable on the other side is the **length of the latus rectum**. To find the value of $a$ with its sign $(+, -)$, express it in the form $y^2 = Ax + By + C$ and take $4a = A \implies a = \frac{A}{4}$.

## TYPE-01: Finding Fundamental Properties from the Equation of a Parabola

> [!important] 01: Find the vertex, focus, axis, directrix, and length of the latus rectum of the parabola $y^2 = 4y + 4x - 8$.
> ![[assets/parabola_type1_math1.png]] [Diagram: Graph of the parabola (y-2)² = 4(x-1)]
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Given equation,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > y^2 &= 4y + 4x - 8 \\
> > \Rightarrow y^2 - 4y + 4 &= 4x - 8 + 4 \\
> > \Rightarrow (y-2)^2 &= 4 \cdot 1 (x-1)
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Comparing the equation with the standard form $Y^2 = 4aX$, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > Y = y - 2, \qquad X = x - 1, \qquad a = 1
> > $$
> > 
> > * **Vertex:** $(X, Y) = (0, 0)$
> >   $$X = 0 \implies x - 1 = 0 \implies x = 1$$
> >   $$Y = 0 \implies y - 2 = 0 \implies y = 2$$
> >   $$\therefore \text{Vertex } = (1, 2)$$
> > 
> > * **Focus:** $(X, Y) = (a, 0)$
> >   $$X = 1 \implies x - 1 = 1 \implies x = 2$$
> >   $$Y = 0 \implies y - 2 = 0 \implies y = 2$$
> >   $$\therefore \text{Focus } = (2, 2)$$
> > 
> > * **Equation of the Axis:** $Y = 0 \implies \boxed{y - 2 = 0}$
> > 
> > * **Equation of the Directrix:** $X = -a \implies x - 1 = -1 \implies \boxed{x = 0}$
> > 
> > * **Length of the Latus Rectum:** $4|a| = 4 \times 1 = \boxed{4 \text{ units}}$

> [!important] 02: Find the fundamental properties of the parabola $x^2 + 4x + 2y = 0$.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Given equation,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > x^2 + 4x + 2y &= 0 \\
> > \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x + 4 &= 4 - 2y \\
> > \Rightarrow (x+2)^2 &= -2(y-2) \\
> > \Rightarrow (x+2)^2 &= 4\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)(y-2)
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Comparing with the standard equation $X^2 = 4aY$, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > X = x + 2, \qquad Y = y - 2, \qquad a = -\frac{1}{2}
> > $$
> > 
> > * **Vertex:** $(X, Y) = (0, 0) \implies x + 2 = 0 \text{ and } y - 2 = 0 \implies \boxed{(-2, 2)}$
> > * **Focus:** $(X, Y) = (0, a)$
> >   $$X = 0 \implies x + 2 = 0 \implies x = -2$$
> >   $$Y = -\frac{1}{2} \implies y - 2 = -\frac{1}{2} \implies y = \frac{3}{2}$$
> >   $$\therefore \text{Focus } = \boxed{\left(-2, \frac{3}{2}\right)}$$
> > * **Equation of the Axis:** $X = 0 \implies \boxed{x + 2 = 0}$
> > * **Equation of the Directrix:** $Y = -a \implies y - 2 = -\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) \implies \boxed{2y - 5 = 0}$

> [!important] 03: Find the fundamental properties of the parabola $5x^2 + 30x + 2y + 59 = 0$.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > First, making the coefficient of $x^2$ equal to $1$, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > x^2 + 6x + \frac{2}{5}y + \frac{59}{5} &= 0 \\
> > \Rightarrow x^2 + 6x + 9 &= -\frac{2}{5}y - \frac{59}{5} + 9 \\
> > \Rightarrow (x+3)^2 &= -\frac{2}{5}y - \frac{14}{5} \\
> > \Rightarrow (x+3)^2 &= -\frac{2}{5}(y+7) \\
> > \Rightarrow (x+3)^2 &= 4\left(-\frac{1}{10}\right)(y+7)
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Comparing with the standard equation $X^2 = 4aY$, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > X = x + 3, \qquad Y = y + 7, \qquad a = -\frac{1}{10}
> > $$
> > 
> > * **Vertex:** $(X, Y) = (0, 0) \implies x = -3, \enspace y = -7 \implies \boxed{(-3, -7)}$
> > * **Focus:** $(X, Y) = (0, a)$
> >   $$X = 0 \implies x + 3 = 0 \implies x = -3$$
> >   $$Y = -\frac{1}{10} \implies y + 7 = -\frac{1}{10} \implies y = -\frac{71}{10}$$
> >   $$\therefore \text{Focus } = \boxed{\left(-3, -\frac{71}{10}\right)}$$
> > * **Equation of the Axis:** $X = 0 \implies \boxed{x + 3 = 0}$
> > * **Equation of the Directrix:** $Y = -a \implies y + 7 = \frac{1}{10} \implies \boxed{10y + 69 = 0}$
> > * **Length of the Latus Rectum:** $4|a| = 4 \times \frac{1}{10} = \boxed{\frac{2}{5} \text{ units}}$

## TYPE-02: Finding the Equation of a Parabola from Given Conditions (Standard Forms)

> [!important] 01: If the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ passes through the point $(0, 5)$ and its vertex is at $(-2, 3)$, find the values of $a, b, c$.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Since the degree of $x$ in the given equation is $2$, the equation of the required parabola with vertex $(-2, 3)$ is,
> > 
> > $$
> > (x+2)^2 = 4A(y-3) \qquad \dots \enspace (i)
> > $$
> > 
> > Since equation $(i)$ passes through $(0, 5)$,
> > 
> > $$
> > (0+2)^2 = 4A(5-3) \implies 8A = 4 \implies A = \frac{1}{2}
> > $$
> > 
> > Substituting the value of $A$ into equation $(i)$, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > (x+2)^2 &= 2(y-3) \\
> > \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x + 4 &= 2(y-3) \\
> > \Rightarrow y - 3 &= \frac{1}{2}x^2 + 2x + 2 \\
> > \Rightarrow y &= \frac{1}{2}x^2 + 2x + 5
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, $\boxed{a = \frac{1}{2}, \enspace b = 2, \enspace c = 5}$

> [!important] 02: Find the equation of the parabola whose vertex is $(4, -3)$, length of the latus rectum is $4$, and axis is parallel to the $x$-axis. *[BUET, Board]*
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Since the axis is parallel to the $x$-axis, the equation of the parabola is,
> > 
> > $$
> > (y+3)^2 = 4a(x-4) \qquad \dots \enspace (i)
> > $$
> > 
> > Given, length of the latus rectum,
> > 
> > $$
> > 4|a| = 4 \implies a = \pm 1
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the required parabola is:
> > 
> > $$
> > \boxed{(y+3)^2 = \pm 4(x-4)}
> > $$

> [!important] 03: Find the equation of the parabola whose vertex is $(4, -3)$, length of the latus rectum is $4$, and directrix is parallel to the $x$-axis.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Since the directrix is parallel to the $x$-axis, the axis will be parallel to the $y$-axis.
> > Therefore, the equation of the required parabola is:
> > 
> > $$
> > \boxed{(x-4)^2 = \pm 4(y+3)}
> > $$

## TYPE-03: Tangent to a Parabola

### Replacement Rules for Finding the Equation of a Tangent at a Point $(x_1, y_1)$ on a Curve
* $x^2 \longrightarrow x \cdot x_1$
* $y^2 \longrightarrow y \cdot y_1$
* $x \longrightarrow \frac{x + x_1}{2}$
* $y \longrightarrow \frac{y + y_1}{2}$
* $xy \longrightarrow \frac{x_1y + xy_1}{2}$

> [!important] 01: Find the equation of the tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 12x$ at the point $(3, 6)$.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Here, the point $(3, 6)$ lies on the parabola because $6^2 = 12 \times 3 = 36$.
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the tangent at $(3, 6)$ is,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > y \cdot y_1 &= 12 \cdot \frac{x + x_1}{2} \\
> > \Rightarrow 6y &= 12 \cdot \frac{x + 3}{2} \\
> > \Rightarrow 6y &= 6(x + 3) \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{y = x + 3}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$

> [!important] 02: Find the equation of the tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 20$ at the point whose abscissa is $2$.
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > When $x = 2$, $2^2 + y^2 = 20 \implies y^2 = 16 \implies y = \pm 4$.
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the tangent at $(2, \pm 4)$ is,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > x \cdot x_1 + y \cdot y_1 &= 20 \\
> > \Rightarrow 2x \pm 4y &= 20 \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{x \pm 2y = 10}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$

### Condition for a Straight Line to be a Tangent to a Parabola

#### 1. Condition for $y = mx + c$ to be a tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$:
$$\boxed{\enspace c = \frac{a}{m} \enspace}$$
* **Point of Contact:** $\left(\frac{a}{m^2}, \frac{2a}{m}\right)$

> [!tip]- Proof
> Let,
> 
> $$
> y^2 = 4ax \qquad \dots \enspace (i)
> $$
> 
> $$
> y = mx + c \qquad \dots \enspace (ii)
> $$
> 
> From $(i)$ and $(ii)$, we get,
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> (mx + c)^2 &= 4ax \\
> \Rightarrow m^2x^2 + 2mcx + c^2 &= 4ax \\
> \Rightarrow m^2x^2 + (2mc - 4a)x + c^2 &= 0
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> 
> Since a tangent touches the parabola at only one point, this quadratic equation will have only one repeated root for $x$. That means the discriminant $D = 0$.
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> (2mc - 4a)^2 - 4 \cdot m^2 \cdot c^2 &= 0 \\
> \Rightarrow 4m^2c^2 - 16amc + 16a^2 - 4m^2c^2 &= 0 \\
> \Rightarrow 16a^2 &= 16amc \\
> \Rightarrow a &= mc \\
> \therefore c &= \frac{a}{m}
> \end{aligned}
> $$

#### 2. Condition for $y = mx + c$ to be a tangent to the parabola $x^2 = 4ay$:
$$\boxed{\enspace c = -am^2 \enspace}$$

> [!tip]- Proof
> Substituting $y = mx + c$ into $x^2 = 4ay$, we get,
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> x^2 &= 4a(mx + c) \\
> \Rightarrow x^2 - 4amx - 4ac &= 0
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> 
> According to the condition for tangency, discriminant $D = 0$,
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> (-4am)^2 - 4 \cdot 1 \cdot (-4ac) &= 0 \\
> \Rightarrow 16a^2m^2 + 16ac &= 0 \\
> \Rightarrow c &= -am^2
> \end{aligned}
> $$

> [!note] Tangent to a Shifted Parabola
> If the parabola is of the form $(y-\beta)^2 = 4a(x-\alpha)$ or $(x-\alpha)^2 = 4a(y-\beta)$, the straight line should also be taken in the form $(y-\beta) = m(x-\alpha) + c$ before applying the condition.

> [!important] 03: Find the equation of the common tangent to the parabolas $y^2 = 4ax$ and $x^2 = 4ay$.
> ![[assets/parabola_common_tangent.png]] [Diagram: Common tangent to the parabolas y² = 4ax and x² = 4ay]
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Let the equation of the common tangent be $y = mx + c$.
> > 
> > * $y = mx + c$ is a tangent to $y^2 = 4ax$ when $c = \frac{a}{m}$.
> > * $y = mx + c$ is a tangent to $x^2 = 4ay$ when $c = -am^2$.
> > 
> > According to the condition,
> > 
> > $$
> > \frac{a}{m} = -am^2 \implies m^3 = -1 \implies m = -1
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the common tangent line is,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > y &= mx + \frac{a}{m} \\
> > \Rightarrow y &= -x - a \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{x + y + a = 0}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > *(For example: the common tangent to $y^2 = 12x$ and $x^2 = 12y$ is $x + y + 3 = 0$)*

## Focal Distance of a Parabola

![[assets/parabola_focal_distance.png]] [Diagram: Focal distance SP = PM = a + x of a point P(x, y) on a parabola]

* **For the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$:**
  $$SP = PM = ZQ = ZA + AQ \implies \boxed{\enspace SP = a + x \enspace}$$
* **For the parabola $x^2 = 4ay$:**
  $$\boxed{\enspace SP = a + y \enspace}$$

> [!important] 01: If the focal distance of a point on the parabola $y^2 = 16x$ is $6$, what are the coordinates of that point?
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Given parabola, $y^2 = 16x = 4 \cdot 4 \cdot x \implies a = 4$.
> > 
> > According to the condition,
> > 
> > $$
> > SP = 6 \implies a + x = 6 \implies 4 + x = 6 \implies x = 2
> > $$
> > 
> > Now, substituting $x = 2$ into the equation of the parabola, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > y^2 = 16 \times 2 = 32 \implies y = \pm 4\sqrt{2}
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the coordinates of the required point are $\boxed{(2, \pm 4\sqrt{2})}$.

> [!important] 02: If the ordinate of a point on the parabola $y^2 = 9x$ is $12$, what is the focal distance of that point?
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Given parabola,
> > 
> > $$
> > y^2 = 9x = 4 \cdot \frac{9}{4} x \qquad \therefore a = \frac{9}{4}
> > $$
> > 
> > Since the ordinate of the point is $y = 12$,
> > 
> > $$
> > 12^2 = 9x \implies 144 = 9x \implies x = 16
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the focal distance is,
> > 
> > $$
> > SP = x + a = 16 + \frac{9}{4} = \boxed{\frac{73}{4}}
> > $$

## TYPE-04: Finding the Equation of a Parabola using Focus-Directrix Property (Oblique/Tilted Parabola)

> [!note] How to Identify and Sketch an Oblique Parabola
> * If both variables $x$ and $y$ are present in the equation of the directrix or axis, it will not be a standard axis-aligned parabola, but a **tilted (oblique) parabola**.
> * Expressing the directrix in the intercept form $\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} = 1$ allows you to find the axis intercepts and easily draw an approximate sketch. For example: $2x - y = 9 \implies \frac{x}{9/2} + \frac{y}{-9} = 1$ and $3x + 4y = 1 \implies \frac{x}{1/3} + \frac{y}{1/4} = 1$.

> [!important] 01: Find the equation of the parabola, the equation of its axis, and the length of its latus rectum given that its focus is $(-8, -2)$ and its directrix is $2x - y - 9 = 0$.
> ![[assets/tilted_parabola_math1.png]] [Diagram: Parabola with focus S(-8, -2) and directrix 2x-y-9=0]
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > **1. Finding the Equation of the Parabola:**
> > Let $P(x, y)$ be any point on the parabola. By definition,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > SP &= PM \\
> > \Rightarrow \sqrt{(x+8)^2 + (y+2)^2} &= \left| \frac{2x - y - 9}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2}} \right| \\
> > \Rightarrow 5\left\{(x+8)^2 + (y+2)^2\right\} &= (2x - y - 9)^2 \\
> > \Rightarrow 5(x^2 + 16x + 64 + y^2 + 4y + 4) &= 4x^2 + y^2 + 81 - 4xy + 18y - 36x \\
> > \Rightarrow 5x^2 + 5y^2 + 80x + 20y + 340 &= 4x^2 + y^2 - 4xy - 36x + 18y + 81 \\
> > \Rightarrow x^2 + 4y^2 + 4xy + 116x + 2y + 259 &= 0 \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{(x+2y)^2 + 116x + 2y + 259 = 0}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > *(Here, since the second-degree terms form a perfect square, i.e., $ab = h^2$, it confirms that the conic is a parabola)*
> > 
> > ---
> > **2. Finding the Equation of the Axis:**
> > The axis is perpendicular to the directrix and passes through the focus $(-8, -2)$.
> > Equation of a line perpendicular to the directrix $2x - y - 9 = 0$:
> > 
> > $$
> > x + 2y + k = 0
> > $$
> > 
> > Since the line passes through $(-8, -2)$,
> > 
> > $$
> > -8 + 2(-2) + k = 0 \implies k = 12
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the axis is: $\boxed{x + 2y + 12 = 0}$
> > 
> > ---
> > **3. Finding the Length of the Latus Rectum:**
> > When $SP$ is the semi-latus rectum, $PM = SZ$ (perpendicular distance from the focus to the directrix).
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > \text{Length of the latus rectum} &= 2 \cdot SP = 2 \cdot SZ \\
> > &= 2 \left| \frac{2(-8) - (-2) - 9}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2}} \right| \\
> > &= 2 \left| \frac{-16 + 2 - 9}{\sqrt{5}} \right| \\
> > &= \boxed{\frac{46}{\sqrt{5}} \text{ units}}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$

> [!important] 02: The focus of a parabola is $(1, -1)$ and the line $x - y + 2 = 0$ is perpendicular to the axis at the vertex. Find the equation of the parabola. *[BUET]*
> ![[assets/tilted_parabola_buet.png]] [Diagram: Focus S(1, -1), tangent at the vertex x-y+2=0, and parallel directrix x-y+k=0]
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > Since the line $x - y + 2 = 0$ is perpendicular to the axis at the vertex $A$ (tangent at the vertex), the directrix will be parallel to this line.
> > Let the equation of the directrix be:
> > 
> > $$
> > x - y + k = 0
> > $$
> > 
> > We know that the perpendicular distance from the focus $S$ to the directrix ($ZS$) is twice the perpendicular distance from the focus to the tangent at the vertex ($AS$). That is,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > ZS &= 2 \cdot AS \\
> > \Rightarrow \left| \frac{1 - (-1) + k}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} \right| &= 2 \left| \frac{1 - (-1) + 2}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} \right| \\
> > \Rightarrow |k + 2| &= 2 \times 4 = 8 \\
> > \Rightarrow k + 2 &= 8 \qquad [\text{Taking the positive sign as the directrix lies on the opposite side of } S \text{ relative to } A] \\
> > \therefore k &= 6
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the directrix is: $x - y + 6 = 0$.
> > 
> > Now, applying $SP = PM$ by the definition of a parabola, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > \sqrt{(x-1)^2 + (y+1)^2} &= \left| \frac{x - y + 6}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} \right| \\
> > \Rightarrow 2(x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 + 2y + 1) &= x^2 + y^2 + 36 - 2xy + 12x - 12y \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{x^2 + y^2 + 2xy - 16x + 16y - 32 = 0}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$

> [!important] 03: Find the equations of the axis and the directrix of a parabola whose focus is $(-1, 1)$ and vertex is $(2, -3)$.
> ![[assets/parabola_vertex_focus_given.png]] [Diagram: Vertex A(2, -3), focus S(-1, 1), and intersection of directrix and axis Z]
> 
> > [!tip]- Solution
> > **1. Equation of the Axis:**
> > The axis passes through the vertex $A(2, -3)$ and the focus $S(-1, 1)$. Therefore, the equation of the axis is,
> > 
> > $$
> > \begin{aligned}
> > \frac{x - 2}{2 - (-1)} &= \frac{y - (-3)}{-3 - 1} \\
> > \Rightarrow \frac{x - 2}{3} &= \frac{y + 3}{-4} \\
> > \Rightarrow -4x + 8 &= 3y + 9 \\
> > \Rightarrow \boxed{4x + 3y + 1 = 0}
> > \end{aligned}
> > $$
> > 
> > ---
> > **2. Equation of the Directrix:**
> > Let the point of intersection of the axis and the directrix be $Z(\alpha, \beta)$. Since the vertex $A(2, -3)$ is the midpoint of $S(-1, 1)$ and $Z(\alpha, \beta)$, we have
> > 
> > $$
> > \frac{\alpha - 1}{2} = 2 \implies \alpha = 5 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{\beta + 1}{2} = -3 \implies \beta = -7
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the coordinates of point $Z$ are $(5, -7)$.
> > 
> > The directrix is perpendicular to the axis $4x + 3y + 1 = 0$ and passes through $Z(5, -7)$.
> > Let the equation of the directrix be:
> > 
> > $$
> > 3x - 4y + k = 0
> > $$
> > 
> > Substituting the point $(5, -7)$ into it, we get,
> > 
> > $$
> > 3(5) - 4(-7) + k = 0 \implies 15 + 28 + k = 0 \implies k = -43
> > $$
> > 
> > Therefore, the equation of the directrix is: $\boxed{3x - 4y - 43 = 0}$
